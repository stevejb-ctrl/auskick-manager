It's Saturday morning. You coach the Bayside Bandicoots, an Under 12 AFL
team, and the game against Sandringham Sharks starts soon. Your team is
already set up in Siren: 17 kids, everyone's available, and the team
plays "Preferred lines" (kids lean to a line but still rotate). You're
standing on the boundary with your phone, a bit rushed.

Your job on game day:

1. From where you've landed, get to this game's lineup and start the
   game. Check the suggested lineup first. Does anything tell you
   whether it keeps your rules and is fair?
2. Before Q1 starts, decide that one kid should stay on the field all
   game (they're short of match practice). Find how to do that.
3. Start the first quarter. Record two goals for your team (pick
   scorers) and a behind for the opposition. Undo one of your goals.
4. A kid rolls an ankle: mark them injured and bring someone on.
5. When the quarter ends, look at the break screen. Do you understand
   the suggested changes and why kids are moving? Make one change of
   your own, then start Q2.

Report usability friction from the boundary:

- Anything you couldn't do quickly with one hand
- Buttons that were too small, too close together, or easy to mis-tap
- Moments you weren't sure whether something had been saved
- Anything the app said that didn't match what you saw

Finish with a bullet list of observations, most important first, each
saying where it happened. Half-finished is fine if you found real issues.
