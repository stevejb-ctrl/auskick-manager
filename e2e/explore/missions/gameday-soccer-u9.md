It's Saturday. You coach Kingsway United, Under 9 football (soccer, MiniRoos),
playing the Coogee Crabs soon. It's 7 a side with a keeper, in two
20-minute halves, and kids sub on and off whenever you like. Your team
plays "Everyone has a turn in goal": one keeper a half, the kids with the
fewest keeper minutes go in first. One of your kids is a keen keeper and
one would rather not go in goal. 10 kids are here.

Your job:

1. Open the game's lineup. Does it look like a 7-a-side soccer team: a
   keeper in goal, kids in defence, midfield and attack, three on the
   bench? Is anything there that belongs to AFL, netball or rugby
   (behinds, zones, quarters, vests, tries)?
2. Check who is in goal and why. Is it clear how Siren picked them? Can
   you change the keeper before kick-off?
3. Start the game and the first half.
4. Sub a bench kid on for a defender, then do the suggested subs when
   the sub card says they're due.
5. Record a goal for Kingsway by one of your attackers, and a goal for
   the Crabs.
6. End the first half. At half-time, check who takes the keeper top for
   the second half, and where last half's keeper plays now.
7. Start the second half.

Report usability friction from a junior football coach's eye:

- Was it obvious who's in goal, and how the keeper changes?
- Did anything read like another sport (words, scores, rules)?
- Could you sub quickly while the ball was in play?
- Taps that felt slow or risky on the sideline

Finish with a bullet list of observations, most important first, each
saying where it happened. Half-finished is fine if you found real issues.
